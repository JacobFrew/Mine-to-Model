          Anthropic and SpaceX

          Two of the biggest new links in the chain. Anthropic is a huge buyer of compute from almost every supplier on the map. SpaceX merged with xAI in February, listed in June, and now sells AI compute from its Memphis Colossus data centers as well as running Starlink and launches.

          Who supplies its compute

          • Amazon: up to $100B over 10 years for about 5 GW of Trainium chips, plus a $5B investment (April 2026).
          • Google: 5 GW of TPU capacity over five years and up to $40B of investment; Broadcom adds 3.5 GW of TPUs from 2027.

          Six ways to play the chain

          The same AI build-out pays different companies at different times. Power producers get paid now under long contracts; reactor developers get paid in the 2030s if they get built. Matching the bet to your timeline matters as much as picking the company.

          Where the chain is tight

          The bottlenecks sit in the middle of each chain, where only a handful of plants in the world do the work. Bottlenecks are where pricing power, and often the upside, comes from.

          93%

          of US reactor uranium is mined abroad

          Deliveries to US plants in 2025 came from Canada (32%), Kazakhstan (28%) and Australia (15%). US mines supplied 7%.

          1 plant

          does commercial enrichment in the US

          Urenco's site in Eunice, New Mexico. Russian enrichers still supplied about 26% of US enrichment in 2025; import waivers run out by the start of 2028.

          2029+

          before new reactors get commercial HALEU

          Most SMR designs need uranium enriched to nearly 20%. Centrus, General Matter and Orano plants come online between 2029 and the early 2030s.

          9.8 GW

          of nuclear signed by tech, mostly not yet flowing

          About 13 deals by mid-2026. The first restart power arrives with Crane in 2027 and Duane Arnold by early 2029.

          >50%

          of TSMC's AI chip packaging goes to Nvidia

          Every advanced AI chip is built by TSMC, and HBM memory from SK hynix and Micron is sold out for 2026.

          Partly driven by data centers. Coal does not fuel nuclear plants; it reaches data centers through the grid.
